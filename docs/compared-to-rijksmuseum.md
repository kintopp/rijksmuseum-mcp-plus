# Compared with the Rijksmuseum

The Rijksmuseum’s online resources are built primarily for browsing and for retrieving individual records. The collection portal pairs a keyword box with fifteen facet filters and sorts results by popularity; the Search API accepts about a dozen parameters covering the usual catalogue fields and returns bare identifiers in a fixed, unranked order, each of which must then be fetched separately.

rijksmuseum-mcp+ indexes a May 2026 snapshot of the same open data, which lets it answer many questions that the museum’s services cannot. Much of this is drawn from new functionality. Works can be searched by meaning, or by what their images show, as well as more conventionally by keyword. The catalogue can be treated as data: provenance becomes a structured query over owners, transfer types, dates and prices; inscriptions and collectors’ marks are parsed and searchable; conservation and technical-examination records can be read; a bibliography lookup can run in reverse, from a publication to the works it discusses; artists can be filtered by gender, birthplace or profession; and statistics and facet counts can be computed over any result set. Works can also be related to one another by similarity along several dimensions, or found by their proximity to a place.

The remaining differences, though, are not of kind but of degree. With rijksmuseum-mcp+, titles, descriptions, inscriptions and wall texts can be searched separately or together, with boolean, phrase and prefix operators, and the results ranked by relevance. Dates can be matched by overlap with or containment in a range rather than by exact year or century. Works can be filtered by height and width. A creator search matches every recorded spelling of a name. Subjects can be matched by Iconclass notation rather than by label, and a search for a region also takes in the places within it.

Needless to say, none of this supersedes the museum’s own data services, which remain the complete, authoritative and current source. And Rijksmuseum still offers a few things rijksmuseum-mcp+ cannot: popularity ranking, filters for works on display and by gallery, exhibitions, events or Asian art school, and search the Research Library's catalogue.
